Phase 2 — Devil's Advocate: WOUNDED

The roadmap survives as an owner-gated Stage A spike charter. It does not survive as evidence of improvement.

  • Attack 1 — the premise has no outcome evidence. Warp publishes no quantified improvement numbers, and the fuel of Warp's loop — detailed human corrections — is here replaced by same-system signals. The loop proposes to learn from itself and call the result improvement.
  • Attack 2 — G-O measures acceptance/stability, not improvement. A merge rate is gameable, there is no quality endpoint, and the comparison windows are not comparable. Passing G-O would show the owner accepts the improver's output and nothing had to be reverted — not that anything got better.
  • Attack 3 — Step 4 contradiction. Three Stage A features were elaborated before their kill criteria existed. The nonconformance was catalogued as debt while Steps 6–7 recorded PASS — the process absorbed its own violation as paperwork.
  • Attack 4 — DEFINED flattens maturity. One status covers capabilities that are shipped-and-running and capabilities that are specified-only; the map reads as more built than it is.
  • Attack 5 — "34/42 eval-linked" is bibliographic, not discharge. AC-17's eval link did not discharge its L2 floor — the link existed while the evidence class did not. There are 0 L3 acceptance criteria, and hostile-implementer samples (AC-37, AC-42, AC-32) are narrower than their headlines suggest.
  • Attack 6 — traceability is concentrated on Stage A. The self-modifying stages B–E carry no acceptance criteria, and beads holds B–E detail beyond the matrix's depth-grading — an authority conflict over which artifact governs.
  • Attack 7 — the same-family verification discount applies to the process too. Authors, verifiers, and panel share a model family; their agreement is weaker evidence than the ceremony implies.
  • Attack 8 — the kill criterion fires only for narrow failure kinds. There is no kill for stall, for slow degradation, or for budget exhaustion.
  • Attack 9 — apparatus disproportionate to the first uncertainties. The cheapest tests (does one dry iteration even run clean?) sit behind the heaviest machinery (a four-round roadmap, 258 backlog records, a project board).
  • Failed attacks (recorded fairly — these did not land): undecided values are not hidden — rulings are open on their face and recommendations are marked PROPOSED; recurrence 3 / probation 5 were not silently decided; the kill criterion can fire; verification was not a rubber stamp — the original wringer changed the design, and Round 1 verification failed once and was corrected before proceeding; some ACs genuinely bite (planted-edit, lockfile, and planted-evidence fixtures); and "just run Ralph manually" fails as an alternative — the shipped skill is unsafe unamended (SKILL.md:66), so the amendment-first design is load-bearing, not ceremony.
